Diffusion-Guided Learning of Graph Barrier Certificates for Safe Distributed Multi-Robot Control

Under Review at ICRA 2027

Abstract

Safe and scalable control of large multi-robot systems (MRS) remains challenging under local sensing and limited actuation. Existing learning-based approaches commonly rely on gradient-based optimization of the current policy to generate pointwise data for supervision, providing limited coverage of the coupled interactions that can arise over finite horizons. We introduce \emph{Graph Barrier Diffusion (GBDiff)}, a framework that combines model-based diffusion with graph barrier certificates to reason about finite-horizon, actuator-constrained multi-robot interactions. GBDiff performs sampling-based optimization over joint control sequences and propagates the sampled controls through the system dynamics, while jointly evaluating interactions among neighboring robots, to generate diverse, dynamically feasible, and actuator-constrained trajectories. These trajectories are used to learn graph barrier certificates and distill diffusion-guided coordination modes into a distributed, safety-aware policy. Diffusion is used only during training, enabling efficient distributed execution without iterative sampling. Simulations across multi-robot systems of increasing dynamical complexity, including high-dimensional, non-control-affine F-16s, together with hardware experiments involving Crazyflie position exchange, demonstrate improved safety, task performance, and scalability relative to existing methods.
